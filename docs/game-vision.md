# Game vision

Michael enters a temple that is simultaneously a machine, a burial place and a living political territory. He reads its architecture, tests it with a cowboy's compact toolkit, handles its strange dead and earns the attention of adult divine women with interests of their own.

## Player promise

Feel clever, physically capable and a little out of your depth. An urn can be a puzzle weight, an ancestor's home or a creature about to unfold. A goddess can admire a daring rescue yet object to the shrine you broke to accomplish it. Competence and character both matter.

The loop is investigate → test a physical hypothesis → traverse/fight/solve → see divine judgment → choose a response → gain or deepen an alliance → use an intervention to reinterpret a familiar space. Each room teaches readable rules before combining them.

Michael is charismatic and resourceful, not an unstoppable gun platform. The dead can be eerie, funny, dignified or pitiable. Divinity should feel beautiful and dangerous. Steampunk detail is concentrated in his practical equipment and select mechanisms, leaving temple culture legible.

## Design boundary

Single-player side-view action-platforming for desktop first, keyboard/mouse and gamepad. Deliberate movement with forgiveness is the initial feel target; precision speedrunning is not the organizing principle. Desktop/Windows is the first validation target. Other platforms and commercial rating strategy are later decisions.

Long-term expeditions and multiple adult partners are supported by narrative contracts, but this foundation scopes only one temple and one relationship arc. No hub economy, crafting, procedural generation, open world, multiplayer or complete romance campaign is authorized here.
